
Nephrology Reference Guide - Educational Overview
===================================================

1. Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
Chronic Kidney Disease is a long-term condition characterized by a gradual loss of kidney function over time.
It is classified into five stages based on the glomerular filtration rate (GFR). The stages range from mild damage
(Stage 1) to complete kidney failure (Stage 5). Management focuses on slowing disease progression, controlling
blood pressure, and managing symptoms such as anemia and fluid retention.

2. Common Symptoms of CKD
- Fatigue and weakness
- Swelling in legs, ankles, or around eyes (edema)
- Nausea and vomiting
- High blood pressure
- Shortness of breath
Patients are advised to monitor their weight daily, reduce sodium intake, and stay hydrated according to clinical advice.

3. Dietary Management
Patients with CKD should follow a kidney-friendly diet that includes:
- Low sodium (less than 2 grams per day)
- Fluid restriction depending on stage (1.0–1.5L per day)
- Limited protein (0.8g per kg body weight)
- Low potassium foods such as apples, cabbage, and cauliflower
- Avoid high phosphorus foods like cheese, nuts, and cola drinks

4. Medications
ACE inhibitors (like lisinopril, enalapril) and ARBs (like losartan) are commonly used to control blood pressure
and reduce protein loss in urine. Diuretics such as furosemide help reduce fluid buildup. Erythropoietin may be
prescribed to manage anemia. Immunosuppressants such as tacrolimus are used post-transplant to prevent rejection.

5. Fluid Retention and Edema
Fluid retention occurs when the kidneys cannot remove excess water and salt. This causes swelling of the legs and
ankles, and sometimes the face. Patients should track their daily weight and report any sudden increase. Reducing
salt intake and following prescribed diuretics are key parts of management.

6. Nephrotic Syndrome
This condition occurs due to damage in the glomeruli, leading to loss of protein in urine. Major symptoms include
swelling, foamy urine, and fatigue. Treatment includes corticosteroids, diuretics, and a low-sodium, moderate-protein
diet. Monitoring urine protein levels helps track disease progress.

7. Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)
AKI is a sudden decline in kidney function, usually due to dehydration, infections, or medications. Recovery depends
on early detection and appropriate treatment. NSAIDs and certain antibiotics should be avoided. Patients are advised
to monitor urine output and stay hydrated unless contraindicated.

8. Hypertensive Nephropathy
Chronic high blood pressure can damage renal arteries, reducing filtration capacity. Managing hypertension through
lifestyle changes and medications is crucial. DASH diet, weight control, and regular exercise are recommended.

9. Diabetic Nephropathy
High blood sugar levels damage the kidneys over time. Tight glucose control, blood pressure management, and
medications such as ACE inhibitors are essential. Patients should undergo annual urine albumin and kidney function tests.

10. Kidney Transplant Care
After a kidney transplant, patients require lifelong immunosuppressants like tacrolimus and prednisolone. Regular
follow-ups include monitoring for infection, graft rejection, and drug side effects. Hygiene, balanced diet, and
medication adherence are critical for long-term graft survival.

11. Dialysis Overview
For end-stage renal disease (Stage 5 CKD), dialysis or kidney transplantation is needed. Hemodialysis removes waste
through a machine, while peritoneal dialysis uses the patient’s abdominal lining. Both require infection prevention and
strict dietary management.

12. Warning Signs Requiring Medical Attention
Patients should seek medical help if they experience:
- Shortness of breath or chest pain
- Severe leg swelling or sudden weight gain
- Confusion or dizziness
- Reduced urine output or change in color
- Persistent nausea or vomiting

13. Lifestyle and Follow-Up
Smoking cessation, maintaining a healthy body weight, and routine follow-ups are key in kidney care. Vaccinations
against hepatitis B and influenza are advised for patients with advanced CKD.

14. Key Patient Instructions
- Monitor blood pressure and record daily
- Weigh yourself every morning at the same time
- Report swelling, decreased urine, or breathlessness
- Take medications exactly as prescribed
- Avoid over-the-counter painkillers without doctor advice
